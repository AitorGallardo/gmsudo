XSaved https://xsaved.com

Published Jun 2026

The bookmark manager X never built.

If you've ever saved a tweet and never found it again, you already know the problem. X bookmarks are one endless list — no search, no way back to that one post you actually needed.

XSaved fixes that. Your saves turn into a proper library you can search, sort, group, and come back to — with your own notes on anything worth a second look. It grew into a family: the extension in your browser, a native iPhone app, and a native Mac app, all backed by one shared sync layer. Save something at your desk and it shows up on your phone on its own.

On X — the Chrome extension

Install it, open X, and your bookmarks become a clean, visual grid that groups by topic on its own. Type a word, a name, or a topic and the whole library narrows on the spot — filter by author, tag, or media type to close in further, and drop a private note on anything you want to remember. It's live today on the Chrome Web Store.

On your iPhone — native iOS app

The same bookmarks, native on iPhone in an image-first grid that feels right at home. Search keeps up as you type, the system share sheet saves from anywhere, and a home-screen widget keeps your library one tap away. It's a SwiftUI app in TestFlight beta now.

On your Mac — native app + sync hub

On the Mac your whole library gets room to spread out: a dashboard that shows what you've saved at a glance, a spacious grid you can sort and filter, and a keyboard palette to jump straight to anything. It's also the hub of the shared sync backend the other apps talk to — native SwiftUI, in TestFlight beta.

How it's built

The extension is local-first: everything lives in the browser's IndexedDB, so there's no server in the loop and nothing to wait on. Full-text search stays instant across tens of thousands of bookmarks by leaning on the right indexes plus a layered cache that reuses work from one keystroke to the next. Optional cloud sync landed in v0.96 for people who want their library on more than one machine — the Mac app acts as the hub, and the iPhone and extension stay in step through it. It's private by default: no account to create, nothing handed to a server you don't control, and sync is always a choice, never a requirement.

Get it

XSaved is live on the Chrome Web Store, with the iPhone and Mac apps in TestFlight beta. Add it, open your bookmarks on X, and your library fills in as it syncs. See it all at xsaved.com.
